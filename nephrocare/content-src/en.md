# ckd_what | encyclopedia | What is kidney failure?
> Kidney failure means the kidneys can no longer clean the blood well enough on their own.
## The role of the kidneys
Healthy kidneys filter about 180 litres of blood every day. They remove waste and extra water, balance salts such as potassium and sodium, help control blood pressure, keep bones strong and help make red blood cells.
## When they fail
When kidney function falls a lot, waste, water and potassium build up in the body. This can cause tiredness, swelling, breathlessness, itching, nausea and high blood pressure.
- The main causes are diabetes and high blood pressure.
- Other causes: kidney inflammation (glomerulonephritis), inherited cysts, kidney stones or obstruction, some medicines.
- Kidney failure is usually silent for a long time: blood and urine tests detect it.
## Treatment
At an advanced stage, the kidneys must be replaced by dialysis (haemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis) or by a kidney transplant. Medicines, diet and fluid control complete the treatment.
kw: kidney failure, renal failure, CKD, insufficiency

# ckd_stages | encyclopedia | The stages of chronic kidney disease
> Doctors use the eGFR (filtration rate) to describe five stages.
## eGFR
The eGFR is calculated from blood creatinine, age and sex. It estimates how many millilitres of blood the kidneys filter per minute.
- Stage 1: eGFR 90 or more, with signs of kidney damage (protein in urine…).
- Stage 2: 60 to 89.
- Stage 3a: 45 to 59; stage 3b: 30 to 44.
- Stage 4: 15 to 29 – time to prepare dialysis or transplant.
- Stage 5: below 15 – end-stage kidney disease, often requiring dialysis.
## Why it matters
Each stage brings specific checks: blood pressure, anaemia, bones, potassium, diet. Slowing the disease (blood pressure and diabetes control, avoiding kidney-toxic medicines) protects the remaining function.
kw: stages, eGFR, GFR, DFG, filtration

# aki_ckd | encyclopedia | Acute and chronic kidney failure
> Acute failure appears in days and may recover; chronic failure develops over months or years.
## Acute kidney injury
It happens suddenly, for example after severe dehydration, a serious infection, a drop in blood pressure, some medicines or a blockage of urine. With quick treatment of the cause, the kidneys often recover, sometimes partly. Temporary dialysis may be needed.
## Chronic kidney disease
It is a slow, lasting loss of function, usually because of diabetes, high blood pressure or a kidney disease. Lost function does not come back, but its decline can be slowed.
## Acute on chronic
A person with chronic disease can have an acute episode (dehydration, anti-inflammatory drugs, infection). Tell your doctor quickly about diarrhoea, vomiting or fever.
kw: acute, chronic, AKI, sudden

# kidney_function | encyclopedia | How do the kidneys work?
> Each kidney contains about one million tiny filters called nephrons.
## Filtering
Blood enters each kidney through the renal artery. In the nephrons, a filter (the glomerulus) lets water and small molecules pass; tubules then take back what the body needs and leave the waste in the urine.
## Other jobs
- Balance of water, sodium, potassium, calcium and phosphorus.
- Control of acidity in the blood.
- Production of erythropoietin (EPO), the hormone that makes red blood cells.
- Activation of vitamin D, needed for strong bones.
- Help in controlling blood pressure.
## In dialysis
Dialysis replaces the filtering and part of the water and salt balance, but not the hormones: that is why patients also need treatment for anaemia and bones.
kw: nephron, filtration, urine, hormone

# dialysis_machine | encyclopedia | How does the dialysis machine work?
> The machine pumps your blood through an artificial kidney and returns it cleaned.
## The circuit
A pump draws blood from the fistula or catheter through tubing into the dialyser. The dialyser contains thousands of very thin hollow fibres. On the other side of their wall flows the dialysis fluid (dialysate), prepared by the machine from purified water and concentrates.
## Cleaning and removing water
- Waste and extra potassium move from the blood to the dialysate (diffusion).
- A pressure difference removes extra water (ultrafiltration). The amount is set by the team from your weight and your dry weight.
- The machine constantly checks pressures, temperature, air bubbles and blood leaks, and sounds an alarm if something is wrong.
## Your part
Arrive on time, weigh yourself correctly and tell the nurse about cramps, dizziness or pain during the session.
kw: machine, dialyser, generator, dialysate

# dialysis_session | encyclopedia | What happens during a dialysis session?
> A haemodialysis session usually lasts about 4 hours, three times a week.
## Before
You are weighed, your blood pressure and temperature are checked, and the nurse examines the fistula and cleans the arm. Two needles are inserted (or the catheter is connected).
## During
The blood circulates in the machine. You can rest, read, pray, or listen to something. Blood pressure is checked regularly. Tell the team at once about cramps, dizziness, nausea, chest pain or itching.
## After
The needles are removed and the puncture points compressed until bleeding stops. You are weighed again. Get up slowly: dizziness is common after a session. Tiredness after dialysis is frequent and usually improves with rest.
kw: session, haemodialysis, hemodialysis, needles, 4 hours

# hyperkalemia | encyclopedia | High potassium (hyperkalaemia)
> Too much potassium in the blood can disturb the heart rhythm and become life-threatening.
## Why it happens
The failed kidneys no longer remove potassium. It rises with potassium-rich foods, a missed or short session, some medicines, constipation or tissue damage.
## Warning signs
Often there are none. Sometimes: muscle weakness, tingling, heavy legs, palpitations, slow or irregular pulse. Very high potassium can stop the heart without warning.
## Prevention
- Never miss or shorten a dialysis session.
- Limit potassium-rich foods and use the soaking and double-boiling method for vegetables.
- Avoid potassium salt substitutes (low-sodium salts).
- Take potassium binders as prescribed and avoid constipation.
- Always check your potassium result with your team.
If you have weakness or palpitations, especially after a missed session or a large potassium intake, seek urgent care.
kw: potassium, hyperkalemia, heart, palpitations

# anemia | encyclopedia | Anaemia in kidney disease
> Anaemia means too few red blood cells; it causes tiredness and breathlessness.
## Causes
The kidneys produce less erythropoietin (EPO), the hormone that tells the bone marrow to make red blood cells. Iron deficiency, blood loss during dialysis, blood tests and inflammation add to it.
## Signs
Tiredness, pale skin, shortness of breath on effort, palpitations, feeling cold, poor concentration.
## Treatment
- EPO injections (epoetin, darbepoetin…) at the dose decided by the team.
- Iron, often intravenous during dialysis, guided by ferritin and TSAT.
- Regular haemoglobin checks: the target is usually moderate, as values that are too high increase risks.
kw: anaemia, anemia, haemoglobin, EPO, tired

# bone_mineral | encyclopedia | Bone and mineral disorders
> Kidney failure upsets calcium, phosphorus, vitamin D and PTH, which weakens bones and hardens blood vessels.
## What happens
Phosphorus builds up, active vitamin D falls and calcium drops. The parathyroid glands react by making more PTH, which takes calcium out of the bones.
## Consequences
Bone pain, fractures, itching, and calcium deposits in blood vessels and the heart.
## What helps
- Limit phosphorus, especially phosphate additives in processed food.
- Take phosphate binders with meals, as prescribed.
- Vitamin D or other medicines prescribed by the team.
- Follow calcium, phosphorus and PTH results regularly.
kw: bones, phosphorus, calcium, PTH, vitamin D, MBD

# itch_fatigue | encyclopedia | Itching and tiredness
> Two very common complaints in dialysis that can often be improved.
## Itching (pruritus)
It may come from dry skin, high phosphorus, high PTH, insufficient dialysis or allergies.
- Moisturise the skin daily with a fragrance-free cream; lukewarm, short showers.
- Follow your phosphate binders and diet.
- Do not scratch until it bleeds: risk of infection.
- Tell your team: there are treatments for itching in dialysis.
## Tiredness
Causes include anaemia, insufficient dialysis, poor sleep, low mood, lack of activity and undernutrition. Note your tiredness in the diary and discuss it: each cause has its own solution.
kw: itching, pruritus, tired, fatigue, skin

# cramps | encyclopedia | Muscle cramps
> Cramps often happen at the end of a session when a lot of water is removed.
## Why
Removing fluid quickly, low blood pressure, low sodium or calcium, or a dry weight set too low.
## During dialysis
Tell the nurse immediately: the machine settings can be adjusted. Stretching the muscle gently helps.
## Prevention
- Limit weight gain between sessions by controlling salt and fluids: less water to remove means fewer cramps.
- Gentle daily stretching and walking.
- Never take quinine or other medicine for cramps without your nephrologist's advice.
kw: cramps, muscle, spasm

# sleep | encyclopedia | Sleep problems
> Many patients sleep badly: insomnia, restless legs or sleep apnoea.
## Common causes
Itching, cramps, restless legs, anxiety, napping during dialysis, too much tea or coffee, breathlessness from fluid overload, sleep apnoea.
## Good habits
- Keep regular times for going to bed and getting up.
- Avoid tea and coffee after mid-afternoon and screens before bed.
- Some daytime activity and daylight.
- A calm, dark room.
## Talk to the team
Restless legs (an irresistible urge to move the legs in the evening) may be linked to iron deficiency. Snoring with breathing pauses requires evaluation. Do not take sleeping pills without medical advice.
kw: sleep, insomnia, restless legs, apnoea

# nutrition_weight | encyclopedia | Nutrition and weight
> Eating enough protein and energy is as important as limiting potassium, phosphorus and salt.
## The risk of undernutrition
Dialysis removes some nutrients, appetite is often low, and diet restrictions can lead to eating too little. Weight loss of muscle and low albumin are linked to more complications.
## Practical advice
- Eat good-quality protein at each meal (meat, fish, eggs) as advised by the dietitian.
- Use olive oil and allowed starchy foods for energy.
- Do not confuse fluid weight with body weight: your dry weight may need to change if you gain or lose real weight.
- Report loss of appetite or weight loss to the team.
kw: weight, undernutrition, appetite, albumin, protein

# activity | encyclopedia | Physical activity
> Regular, adapted activity improves strength, mood, sleep and blood pressure.
## What to do
- Walking 20 to 30 minutes most days, at a pace where you can still talk.
- Light strength exercises (getting up from a chair, light weights).
- Stretching and balance exercises to avoid falls.
- Some centres offer pedalling during dialysis.
## Precautions
- Do not carry heavy loads or press on the fistula arm.
- Avoid effort right after a session if you feel dizzy.
- Stop and seek advice in case of chest pain, unusual breathlessness or palpitations.
- Ask your doctor before starting, especially with heart disease or diabetes.
kw: sport, exercise, walking, activity

# travel | encyclopedia | Travelling on dialysis
> Travel is possible with good planning: sessions must continue wherever you go.
## Before leaving
- Book sessions in a dialysis centre at your destination several weeks in advance (often 1 to 2 months).
- Ask your centre for a medical file: latest tests, serology, dry weight, prescription, dialysis settings.
- Take enough medicines for the whole trip plus a few days, in hand luggage.
## During the trip
- Keep your diet and fluid limits: heat, holidays and celebrations make it harder.
- In hot weather, avoid dehydration and also fluid overload.
- Keep emergency numbers and the centre's address with you.
kw: travel, holiday, journey, trip

# mental_health | encyclopedia | Mental health
> Sadness, anxiety and discouragement are frequent and deserve care like any other symptom.
## Recognise it
Persistent sadness, loss of interest, irritability, sleep or appetite changes, feeling of being a burden, dark thoughts.
## What helps
- Talk about it with your nephrologist, nurse, a psychologist or a social worker.
- Stay in touch with family and friends; share tasks.
- Keep activities you enjoy and adapt them.
- Faith, prayer and meeting other patients can bring comfort.
If you have thoughts of harming yourself, seek help immediately from a doctor or emergency services.
kw: depression, anxiety, mood, psychology

# daily_life | encyclopedia | Daily life with dialysis
> Organisation makes it easier to combine treatment, family and work.
## Organise your week
- Note your sessions, medicines and appointments (the app can remind you).
- Prepare your bag the evening before.

## Work and studies
Many patients continue to work or study, sometimes with adapted hours. Talk to your employer and social services about your rights.

# nut_basics | nutrition | The dialysis diet: the basics
> Five points to watch: potassium, phosphorus, salt, fluids and enough protein.
## Why a special diet?
Between sessions, the body cannot remove potassium, phosphorus, salt and water. What you eat and drink accumulates until the next dialysis.
## The general principles
- Potassium: choose low-potassium fruit and vegetables and prepare vegetables to remove part of it.
- Phosphorus: limit dairy, processed foods and drinks with phosphate additives; take binders with meals.
- Salt: cook without added salt, avoid stock cubes and salty products.
- Fluids: respect the allowance given by your team.
- Protein: eat enough, as dialysis increases needs.
## Personalised
These rules depend on your tests, your remaining urine, diabetes, heart and weight. Your dietitian adapts them to you.
kw: diet, regime, food, basics

# nut_potassium | nutrition | Potassium in food
> Potassium is found in most fruit, vegetables, pulses, nuts and chocolate.
## Foods rich in potassium
Bananas, dates, dried fruit (apricots, figs, raisins), melon, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes and tomato paste, spinach, artichokes, dried beans, lentils, chickpeas, nuts, chocolate, coffee in large amounts, fruit juices, potassium salt substitutes.
## Lower-potassium choices
Apples, pears, pineapple, berries in small portions, cucumber, lettuce, onion, green pepper, green beans, white rice, pasta, couscous, white bread.
## Tips
- Portion size matters: a small portion of a medium-potassium food is better than a large one.
- Soak and boil vegetables and discard the water.
- Do not drink cooking water or the juice of canned fruit.
kw: potassium, fruit, vegetables, banana, dates

# nut_leaching | nutrition | How to reduce potassium in vegetables
> Soaking and double boiling can remove a large part of the potassium from potatoes and vegetables.
## The method
- Peel and cut the vegetables into small pieces or thin slices.
- Rinse in warm water.
- Soak in a large volume of warm water (about 10 times the volume of vegetables) for at least 2 hours, ideally longer, changing the water once.
- Boil in a large amount of fresh water for a few minutes, discard the water.
- Boil again in fresh water until cooked, then discard this water too.
## Good to know
- Steaming, microwave and frying keep the potassium.
- Never use the cooking water for soup or sauce.
- Even after soaking, the portion size remains important.
- Frozen vegetables, already blanched, contain a little less potassium.
kw: soaking, leaching, double boiling, potatoes, vegetables

# nut_phosphorus | nutrition | Phosphorus in food
> Phosphorus is found in protein foods, dairy and above all in food additives.
## Main sources
- Dairy: milk, cheese (especially processed and melted cheese), yogurt.
- Processed meats, ready meals, sausages (merguez, cachir), canned fish with bones.
- Cola drinks, some baking powders and industrial cakes.
- Nuts, chocolate, wholegrain cereals, pulses.
## Additives
Phosphate additives (E338 to E343, E450 to E452 and others) are absorbed almost completely. Read labels and choose fresh, home-cooked food.
## Binders
Phosphate binders must be taken during the meal (not before or after) to work. Your doctor decides the dose.
kw: phosphorus, phosphate, additives, dairy, cheese

# nut_sodium | nutrition | Salt and sodium
> Salt makes you thirsty, raises blood pressure and increases weight gain between sessions.
## Hidden salt
Bread, stock cubes, olives, harissa, cheese, cold meats, canned food, crisps, ready-made sauces and soups contain a lot of salt.
## How to cook with less salt
- Do not add salt at the table and use little when cooking.
- Season with garlic, onion, lemon, vinegar, herbs (parsley, coriander, mint), cumin, paprika, pepper.
- Avoid stock cubes; make your own broth.
- Soak olives and change the water.
- Never use potassium salt substitutes.
## Result
Less salt means less thirst, better blood pressure and fewer cramps during dialysis.
kw: salt, sodium, thirst, blood pressure

# nut_protein | nutrition | Protein: eat enough
> On haemodialysis, protein needs increase; many patients do not eat enough.
## Why
Each session removes amino acids. Lack of protein leads to muscle loss, weakness, infections and low albumin.
## Good sources
Meat, chicken, fish, eggs (egg white is rich in protein and low in phosphorus). The dietitian tells you how much you need, often about 1.2 g per kg of body weight per day in haemodialysis.
## Balance with phosphorus
Protein foods also contain phosphorus: favour fresh meat, fish and egg whites over cheese and processed meats, and take binders with meals.
## Before dialysis
For patients not yet on dialysis, the advice is often different (sometimes less protein). Follow your own team's advice.
kw: protein, meat, eggs, albumin, muscle

# nut_fluids | nutrition | Fluids and thirst
> Without urine, everything you drink stays in the body until the next session.
## Why limit fluids
Too much fluid causes swelling, high blood pressure, shortness of breath and strain on the heart. Large weight gains also make dialysis harder (cramps, low blood pressure).
## Your allowance
The team often sets a daily allowance based on your remaining urine (for example 500 ml plus the volume of urine). Only follow the allowance given to you.
## Tips against thirst
- Eat less salt: it is the main cause of thirst.
- Use small cups and spread drinks over the day.
- Suck an ice cube (count it) or a slice of lemon; chew sugar-free gum.
- Take medicines with meals or with a little liquid.
- Count soup, yogurt, ice cream and watery fruit.
kw: fluids, water, thirst, drink, weight gain

# nut_labels | nutrition | Reading food labels
> Labels help you find hidden salt, phosphorus and potassium.
## Salt
Look at "salt" or "sodium" per 100 g. As a guide, more than 1.25 g of salt (0.5 g sodium) per 100 g is a lot; less than 0.3 g of salt is little.
## Phosphorus
Look for words containing "phosph" in the ingredients list and the codes E338–E343, E450–E452. Choose products without them.
## Potassium
Avoid products with "potassium chloride" (often in "light" or "low-sodium" salts and some soups).
## Ingredients order
Ingredients are listed from the largest to the smallest amount. Short lists of simple ingredients are generally better.
kw: label, additives, E-number, ingredients

# fis_what | fistula | What is an arteriovenous fistula?
> It is the best access for haemodialysis: your "lifeline".
## Definition
A surgeon connects an artery to a vein in the arm, under local or regional anaesthesia. The strong arterial flow makes the vein grow larger and its wall stronger, so it can be punctured three times a week.
## Maturation
A new fistula needs about 6 to 8 weeks (sometimes more) before it can be used. Exercises such as squeezing a soft ball may be advised by the team.
## Why it is preferred
Compared with a catheter, the fistula causes fewer infections and fewer blockages and gives better dialysis. Some patients have a graft (prosthesis) instead; the care is similar.
kw: fistula, FAV, AV fistula, access

# fis_care | fistula | Taking care of your fistula every day
> Simple daily gestures help your fistula last for years.
## Every day
- Check the thrill (buzz) morning and evening.
- Keep the arm clean; wash it before each session with soap and water.
- Watch for redness, swelling, pain or discharge.
## Protect it
- No blood pressure measurement, blood test or drip on the fistula arm.
- No tight clothes, watch, bracelet or bag strap on this arm.
- Do not sleep on the arm.
- Avoid carrying heavy loads with this arm.
## After the session
Keep the dressings for a few hours as advised, then remove them gently. Do not scratch the scabs.
kw: fistula care, arm, hygiene

# fis_thrill | fistula | Checking the thrill and the bruit
> Feeling the thrill each day is the simplest way to know the fistula is working.
## How to check
Place your fingertips on the fistula, near the scar: you should feel a continuous vibration, like a buzzing or a cat purring. This is the thrill. Putting your ear (or a stethoscope) close, you can hear a whooshing noise: the bruit.
## When to worry
- The thrill is absent or much weaker than usual.
- The bruit has become a sharp, pulsating beat.
- The fistula is hard, painful or the hand is cold and pale.
In these cases call your dialysis centre the same day: a blocked fistula can often be reopened if treated within hours.
kw: thrill, bruit, vibration, buzz

# fis_hygiene | fistula | Hygiene and skin care of the arm
> Clean skin reduces the risk of infection at the puncture sites.
## Before each session
Wash the arm with soap and water and dry it well. Do not apply cream on the puncture area before the session.
## Between sessions
- Moisturise the skin outside the puncture points if it is dry.
- Do not scratch; keep nails short.
- Avoid injuries, burns and insect bites on this arm.
- Wear clean, loose clothing.
## Showers
You can shower normally after the dressings have been removed. Avoid soaking in public baths or the sea while the punctures are fresh.
kw: hygiene, washing, skin, arm

# fis_protect | fistula | Protecting the fistula arm
> Anything that squeezes the arm can slow the flow and cause a blockage.
## To avoid
- Blood pressure cuffs, tourniquets, blood tests and drips on this arm (tell every nurse and doctor).
- Watches, bracelets, tight sleeves.
- Sleeping on the arm or with the arm folded under the head.
- Carrying heavy shopping bags or weights with this arm.
- Hard blows: protect the arm during work or sport.
## Blood pressure too low
Very low blood pressure, dehydration (diarrhoea, vomiting, heat) can also block the fistula. Contact the team in these situations.
kw: protect, arm, compression, tight

# fis_infection | fistula | Signs of infection
> An infection of the fistula must be treated quickly.
## Signs
- Redness, heat, swelling or pain around the fistula.
- Pus or discharge at a puncture point.
- Fever, chills, especially during or after dialysis.
## What to do
Contact your dialysis centre the same day. Do not put any home remedy on the arm. If you have high fever, chills or feel very unwell, go to the emergency department: an infection can spread to the blood.
kw: infection, redness, pus, fever

# fis_thrombosis | fistula | Thrombosis and blockage
> A blood clot can block the fistula; quick treatment can save it.
## Signs
The thrill and bruit disappear, the vein becomes hard, sometimes painful.
## Causes
Low blood pressure, dehydration, compression of the arm, narrowing (stenosis) of the vein.
## What to do
Call the dialysis centre or go to the hospital immediately, even at night or on a weekend. Do not wait for the next session: declotting is easier within the first hours. Do not massage the fistula hard.
kw: thrombosis, clot, blocked, no thrill

# fis_bleeding | fistula | Bleeding from the fistula
> Bleeding after a session is usually stopped by firm, gentle pressure.
## What to do
- Press directly on the bleeding point with a clean compress or cloth, with two fingers, firmly but without blocking the flow completely.
- Keep pressing for 10 to 15 minutes without looking.
- Raise the arm if possible.
## When it is an emergency
If the bleeding is heavy, spurts, or does not stop after 15–20 minutes of pressure, call emergency services and keep pressing until help arrives. Never put a tourniquet on the arm.
## Prevention
Tell the team if bleeding after needle removal is often long: the needles, the anticoagulant dose or a narrowing may need checking.
kw: bleeding, blood, haemorrhage, compress

# em_chest_pain | emergency | Chest pain
> Chest pain can be a heart attack: call emergency services immediately.
## Danger signs
- Pain, pressure or tightness in the chest, sometimes spreading to the arm, jaw or back.
- With sweating, nausea, shortness of breath or feeling faint.
## What to do
- Call emergency services (14 in Algeria, 112 in Europe) without delay.
- Stop any effort, sit or lie down.
- If you are at the dialysis centre, tell the nurse at once.
Diabetic and dialysis patients may have less typical pain: unusual tiredness or breathlessness must also be taken seriously.
kw: chest pain, heart attack, angina

# em_breathing | emergency | Shortness of breath
> Breathlessness is often due to too much fluid in the lungs.
## Danger signs
- Difficulty breathing, especially when lying down; need to sleep sitting up.
- Cough with frothy sputum, rapid weight gain, swelling of the legs.
## What to do
- If breathing is very difficult: call emergency services immediately.
- Otherwise call your dialysis centre: an extra session may be needed.
- Sit upright, do not drink more, avoid salt.
kw: breathless, breathing, lungs, oedema, fluid overload

# em_bleeding | emergency | Severe bleeding
> Heavy bleeding from the fistula or elsewhere needs immediate pressure and help.
## What to do
- Press firmly on the bleeding point with a clean cloth and do not let go.
- Lie down if you feel faint.
- Call emergency services if the bleeding is heavy or does not stop after 15 minutes.
- Do not apply a tourniquet on the fistula arm.
## Other bleeding
Black stools, vomiting blood or prolonged nosebleeds also need urgent medical advice, especially if you take anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs.
kw: bleeding, haemorrhage, blood

# em_unconscious | emergency | Fainting and loss of consciousness
> A person who does not respond needs immediate emergency care.
## If someone loses consciousness
- Call emergency services.
- Check breathing. If they breathe, place them on their side (recovery position).
- If they do not breathe normally, start chest compressions if you know how, following the operator's instructions.
## Feeling faint
Dizziness and near-fainting after dialysis are often due to low blood pressure: lie down with the legs raised. If it does not improve quickly, or if there is chest pain, confusion or weakness of one side of the body, call emergency services. Low blood sugar in diabetic patients can also cause fainting.
kw: fainting, unconscious, collapse, dizziness

# em_no_thrill | emergency | The fistula thrill has stopped
> No thrill means the fistula may be blocked: act the same day.
## What to do
- Check again in a warm room, with the arm relaxed.
- If you still feel nothing, call your dialysis centre or go to the hospital immediately.
- Do not wait for the next session.
## Why urgency
A clot can often be removed (declotting) in the first hours. The longer you wait, the lower the chance of saving the fistula, and a new access may be needed.
kw: thrill, fistula blocked, thrombosis

# em_infection | emergency | Fever and signs of infection
> Infections can become serious quickly in dialysis patients.
## Danger signs
- Fever above 38 °C, chills, especially during dialysis.
- Redness, pus or pain at the fistula or catheter.
- Confusion, very low blood pressure, fast breathing.
## What to do
Contact your centre the same day. With chills, confusion or feeling very unwell, go to the emergency department. With a catheter, fever is always urgent. Do not take antibiotics left at home without advice.
kw: fever, infection, chills, catheter

# em_hyperkalemia | emergency | Signs of dangerously high potassium
> Weakness and palpitations after a missed session can mean dangerous potassium.
## Danger signs
- Unusual muscle weakness, heavy legs, tingling.
- Palpitations, slow or irregular pulse.
- Especially after a missed or shortened session, or a meal very rich in potassium.
## What to do
Go to the emergency department or your dialysis centre immediately: an urgent blood test, an ECG and often a dialysis session are needed. Do not wait for symptoms to worsen.
kw: potassium, hyperkalaemia, palpitations, weakness

# bp_high | bp | High blood pressure
> High blood pressure is frequent in dialysis and is often linked to excess salt and fluid.
## Why it matters
It tires the heart and damages blood vessels, increasing the risk of heart attack and stroke.
## What helps
- Reduce salt and respect your fluid allowance: this lowers weight gain and blood pressure.
- Reach your dry weight at the end of sessions, as set by the team.
- Take blood pressure medicines exactly as prescribed; some are taken in the evening or not before dialysis according to the doctor.
- Regular activity, no smoking.
## Danger signs
Very high values with severe headache, chest pain, breathlessness, confusion or blurred vision require emergency care.
kw: hypertension, high blood pressure

# bp_low | bp | Low blood pressure during and after dialysis
> A drop in blood pressure during the session is common when a lot of water is removed.
## Signs
Dizziness, yawning, nausea, cramps, sweating, blurred vision, feeling faint.
## What to do
- During the session: tell the nurse immediately; the machine can be adjusted and your legs raised.
- At home: lie down with your legs raised; get up slowly.
## Prevention
- Limit weight gain between sessions (less salt, fewer fluids).
- Ask whether to take blood pressure medicines before the session.
- Avoid large meals during dialysis if advised.
- Tell the team if it happens often: the dry weight may need to be reassessed.
kw: hypotension, low blood pressure, dizziness

# bp_measure | bp | Measuring blood pressure correctly
> A good measurement depends on good conditions.
## How
- Rest seated for 5 minutes, back supported, feet flat, legs not crossed.
- Use the arm without the fistula, with a cuff of the right size at heart level.
- Do not talk during the measurement.
- Take two measurements one minute apart and record both or the average.
## When
At home, morning and evening on several days, as asked by your doctor. Note the context (before or after dialysis, at home) in the app.
kw: measure, blood pressure monitor, cuff

# med_general | meds | Medicines in dialysis: general rules
> Dialysis patients often take many medicines; each has a precise role.
## Good practices
- Keep an up-to-date list of all your medicines and show it to every doctor and pharmacist.
- Take them at the times indicated, even if you feel well.
- Some medicines are removed by dialysis: your doctor tells you whether to take them after the session.
- Never stop, change or add a medicine without medical advice.
## Self-medication
Many common medicines and herbal products are dangerous for kidney patients. Always ask your doctor or pharmacist first.
kw: medicines, treatment, prescription

# med_binders | meds | Phosphate binders
> They trap the phosphorus from food in the gut so that it is eliminated in the stools.
## Examples
Calcium carbonate or acetate, sevelamer, lanthanum, iron-based binders. The type and dose are chosen by your doctor.
## How to take them
- During the meal (at the beginning or middle), not on an empty stomach.
- With every meal or snack containing phosphorus, as prescribed.
- If you skip a meal, ask your team whether to skip the binder.
## Side effects
Constipation, bloating or nausea are possible: tell your team rather than stopping on your own.
kw: binders, phosphate, calcium carbonate, sevelamer

# med_epo | meds | EPO (erythropoietin) injections
> EPO replaces the hormone that the kidneys no longer make enough of, to treat anaemia.
## How it is given
By injection under the skin or into the dialysis line, once or several times a week or month, depending on the product.
## Follow-up
The dose is adjusted by the team according to your haemoglobin. Too high a haemoglobin increases certain risks, so regular tests are essential.
## To know
EPO works well only if there is enough iron. It can raise blood pressure: measure it regularly. Keep the pens in the refrigerator if you have them at home.
kw: EPO, erythropoietin, anaemia, injection

# med_iron | meds | Iron
> Iron is needed to make red blood cells; it is often given into the vein during dialysis.
## Why
Dialysis patients lose iron (blood tests, blood left in the circuit) and absorb it poorly from food and tablets.
## Follow-up
Ferritin and TSAT guide the treatment. The team decides doses and timing.
## Tablets
If iron tablets are prescribed, they may darken the stools and cause constipation. Do not take them at the same time as phosphate binders unless your doctor says so.
kw: iron, ferritin, TSAT, anaemia

# med_vitd | meds | Vitamin D and treatments for PTH
> These medicines help control calcium, phosphorus and parathyroid hormone to protect bones.
## Examples
Native vitamin D, active vitamin D (alfacalcidol, calcitriol), cinacalcet or other calcimimetics.
## Follow-up
Doses depend on calcium, phosphorus and PTH results. Take them exactly as prescribed; never add vitamins or calcium on your own.
kw: vitamin D, PTH, alfacalcidol, cinacalcet

# med_bp_drugs | meds | Blood pressure medicines
> They protect the heart and vessels; their timing around dialysis matters.
## Points to know
- Some are taken in the evening or not before a session to avoid low blood pressure during dialysis: follow your doctor's schedule.
- Do not stop them suddenly.
- Report dizziness or very low values.
kw: antihypertensive, blood pressure medicine

# med_avoid | meds | Medicines and products to avoid without advice
> Some common products are dangerous when the kidneys do not work.
## To avoid unless prescribed
- Anti-inflammatory drugs (ibuprofen, diclofenac, high-dose aspirin…): they harm the remaining kidney function and raise potassium.
- Potassium salt substitutes and some "light" salts.
- Laxatives and enemas containing phosphate or magnesium.
- Some antacids containing aluminium or magnesium.
- Herbal teas and traditional remedies of unknown composition, some of which are toxic to the kidneys or rich in potassium.
- Vitamin and mineral supplements not prescribed.
## For pain
Paracetamol at the dose advised by your doctor is usually preferred, but ask first.
kw: avoid, ibuprofen, anti-inflammatory, herbal, self-medication

# faq_cure | faq | Can my kidneys recover?
> In chronic kidney failure, lost function does not come back; in acute failure, it often recovers.
Dialysis does not heal the kidneys: it replaces part of their work. Some patients with acute failure recover and stop dialysis. In chronic disease, the options are long-term dialysis or a kidney transplant. Ask your nephrologist about your own situation and about transplantation.

@lab UREA | Urea
what: Urea is a waste product of protein breakdown, removed by the kidneys. In dialysis it is measured before and after the session to check how well the dialysis cleans the blood (Kt/V, urea reduction ratio).
high: A high pre-dialysis urea can mean insufficient dialysis, a large protein intake, bleeding in the gut or dehydration. It can cause nausea, loss of appetite and itching. Your team interprets it with the other results.
low: A low urea in a dialysis patient can be a sign of eating too little protein (undernutrition). Talk to the dietitian.
tips: Urea always looks high in kidney failure; what matters is its evolution and the dialysis adequacy calculated by the team.

@lab CREAT | Creatinine
what: Creatinine comes from the muscles and is removed by the kidneys. It is used to estimate kidney function (eGFR) before dialysis.
high: In dialysis, creatinine is always high; it depends on muscle mass and dialysis. A rise before dialysis indicates worsening kidney function.
low: A falling creatinine in a dialysis patient may reflect loss of muscle mass.
tips: Do not compare your creatinine with the normal laboratory range when you are on dialysis.

@lab K | Potassium
what: Potassium is essential for nerves, muscles and the heart. The failed kidneys no longer remove it.
high: High potassium (hyperkalaemia) can cause muscle weakness and dangerous heart rhythm disturbances. Causes: potassium-rich foods, missed sessions, some medicines, constipation. A very high value requires urgent advice.
low: Low potassium is less common in dialysis; it can occur with vomiting, diarrhoea or poor appetite and can also affect the heart.
tips: Check your potassium with your team and adapt your diet; use soaking and double boiling for vegetables.

@lab PHOS | Phosphorus
what: Phosphorus is a mineral found in food. When it accumulates, it weakens the bones and hardens the blood vessels.
high: High phosphorus causes itching and, over time, bone disease and calcification of vessels and the heart. Limit dairy and processed foods with additives and take binders during meals.
low: Low phosphorus may reflect undernutrition or too much binder; tell your team.
tips: Labs report phosphorus in mmol/L or mg/L: the app converts both.

@lab CA | Calcium
what: Calcium is needed for bones, muscles and the heart. It is regulated by vitamin D, PTH and phosphorus.
high: High calcium can come from calcium-based binders or vitamin D and can favour vessel calcification. The team may adjust treatment.
low: Low calcium may cause tingling and cramps and stimulates PTH.
tips: Calcium must be interpreted with albumin, phosphorus and PTH.

@lab HB | Haemoglobin
what: Haemoglobin carries oxygen in the red blood cells. It is low in anaemia.
high: Too high a haemoglobin under EPO treatment can increase the risk of thrombosis and stroke; the team then reduces the dose.
low: Low haemoglobin causes tiredness, breathlessness and palpitations. Treatment includes EPO and iron.
tips: In dialysis, the target is usually moderate (often around 10 to 11.5 g/dL), set by your nephrologist.

@lab FERRITIN | Ferritin
what: Ferritin reflects the iron reserves in the body.
high: High ferritin can mean enough or too much iron, but also inflammation or infection. The team interprets it with TSAT and CRP.
low: Low ferritin means lack of iron, which limits the effect of EPO.
tips: Iron is often given intravenously during dialysis according to ferritin and TSAT.

@lab ALB | Albumin
what: Albumin is the main protein in the blood. It reflects nutrition and inflammation.
high: A high albumin is rare and usually due to dehydration.
low: Low albumin is linked to undernutrition, inflammation and more complications. Eat enough protein and energy and talk to your dietitian.
tips: Albumin is one of the most important nutrition markers in dialysis.

@lab PTH | Parathyroid hormone (PTH)
what: PTH is made by the parathyroid glands and controls calcium and phosphorus. It rises in kidney failure.
high: Very high PTH takes calcium from the bones (fragile bones, pain, itching) and may need vitamin D, calcimimetics or sometimes surgery.
low: Very low PTH can lead to "adynamic" bone, also fragile; the team may reduce some treatments.
tips: In dialysis, the target is wider than the normal range (often 2 to 9 times the upper normal limit).

@lab CRP | CRP (C-reactive protein)
what: CRP is a marker of inflammation and infection.
high: A high CRP suggests infection or inflammation (fistula, catheter, teeth, lungs…). With fever, contact your team quickly.
low: A low CRP is normal and good news.
tips: Chronic inflammation can worsen anaemia and nutrition.

@lab TSAT | Transferrin saturation (TSAT)
what: TSAT shows how much iron is available to make red blood cells.
high: High TSAT may indicate too much iron; the team adjusts iron treatment.
low: Low TSAT means iron is lacking for red blood cells, even if ferritin is normal.
tips: Read TSAT together with ferritin.
